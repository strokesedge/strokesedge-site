TPC Craig Ranch is a different puzzle than Colonial. Par 72 with four par 5s means distance genuinely matters here, and the regression weights reflect it — SG: Off the Tee runs closer to the middle of the weighting structure at Craig Ranch, not near zero like at Colonial.

Bermudagrass greens rather than bentgrass changes the putting profile entirely. Putting on Bermuda rewards a different stroke pattern than bentgrass, and players who excel specifically on Bermuda surfaces see a different model treatment than their overall SG: Putting numbers suggest. The model uses surface-specific putting stats wherever available.

The fairways at Craig Ranch are wider than Colonial, which reduces the Driving Accuracy weight and shifts more value toward distance and approach quality in the 150-200 yard band. Four par 5s create legitimate eagle opportunities for longer hitters, amplifying the OTT signal compared to a venue like Colonial or Augusta.

Wind is a genuine factor at Craig Ranch. North Texas wind plays in full from the exposed back nine, and the model incorporates a wind resistance multiplier on the approach stats for players who grade below average in their ball flight trajectory data. That adjustment is not visible in the basic rankings but sits inside the L1 regression output.

The contrast with Colonial is stark and intentional. OTT at 20% versus Colonial's 3% reflects the structural role of driving distance when four par 5s are in the rotation. The par-5 scoring rate is the biggest swing stat at Craig Ranch, and it heavily rewards players who can reach greens in two consistently.

ARG at 14% is also meaningfully higher than Colonial's 5%. Bermudagrass rough and the grain pattern around the greens creates short-game situations that genuinely separate players. The scrambling component has a cleaner signal here than at tighter, approach-dominant venues.
