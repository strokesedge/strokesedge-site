TPC Southwind · Par 70 · 7,288 Yards · Aug 13–16, 2026

The 2026 FedEx St. Jude Championship opened the FedExCup Playoffs at TPC Southwind, a Ron Prichard design where water touches 11 of 18 holes and the fairways rank among the Tour's narrowest. This was only the second tournament on a materially different golf course — every green was rebuilt ahead of 2025 and the layout picked up roughly 50 yards. What hasn't changed is the winner profile: every champion since the event moved here in 2019 has been an elite iron player that week, sometimes despite an inconsistent driver.

01 — Course Overview

Southwind fingerprint.

TPC Southwind plays to a par 70 at 7,288 yards for the 2026 FedEx St. Jude Championship (verified against pgatour.com's current tournament listing). The course opened in 1988, designed by Ron Prichard with Hubert Green and Fuzzy Zoeller consulting, and has hosted this event almost continuously since 1989 — first as the St. Jude Classic, then a WGC event (2019-2021), and since 2022 as the FedExCup Playoffs opener.

What stats matter this week: SG: Approach is the model's top factor by a clear margin. Southwind's fairways rank among the Tour's narrowest, but the winner sample says approach quality, not driving accuracy, has separated recent champions.

2026 course status — material change, flagged for review
TPC Southwind rebuilt and resurfaced all 18 greens ahead of the 2025 tournament (first resurfacing since 2004) and added roughly 50 yards, most documented on the par-4 5th (485 to 529 yards). Pgatour.com's current listing shows 7,288 yards for 2026; Wikipedia still shows the stale pre-renovation figure of 7,244 yards and doesn't mention the renovation. 2026 was only the second tournament on this setup — Course History and Course Experience weightings discounted pre-2025 Southwind starts accordingly, the same way a fresh restoration is handled at any other venue.

Conditions that week: mid-August in Memphis typically runs hot and humid, with the freshly rebuilt greens still firming up into their second season. The no-cut, 70-player format meant every player in the field played all four rounds regardless of Thursday's score.

Historical winning scores: recent FedEx St. Jude winners at Southwind include Justin Rose at -16 in 2025 (the first tournament on the rebuilt greens) and Hideki Matsuyama at -17 in 2024 (full table in Section 03 below). The one constant across seven winners since 2019 isn't the winning number — it's that every champion had an elite approach-play week.

02 — Predictive Stats

What the data says actually matters.

The qualitative course-fit read from Southwind's winner history and shot data. Exact numeric model weights ran in the Substack workbook.

#1 — SG: Approach
The clearest signal at Southwind. Every winner since the event moved here in 2019 — Koepka, Thomas, Ancer, Zalatoris, Glover, Matsuyama, Rose — had an elite iron-play week. Several won despite an inconsistent driver that week. This is the one trait that hasn't missed.
#2 — Scrambling / Approach From the Rough
Southwind ranks inside the Tour's top six for both penalty from missed fairways and approach difficulty from the rough, on some of the Tour's narrowest fairways. Missing the fairway is common even for accurate players; recovering from it is the differentiator.
#3 — Short-Iron Proximity (inside 175 yards)
Close to two-thirds of approach shots from the fairway come from inside 175 yards. A general SG: Approach number blends every distance band; short-iron and wedge proximity specifically is the more targeted read on fit here.
Overweighted by the market — Driving Accuracy
Narrow fairways make accuracy look like the top predictor, and the market priced it that way. The actual winner sample doesn't support it — recent champions have combined shaky driving with elite approach play and still won.
Course History & Course Experience — discounted pre-2025 starts
Southwind changed materially ahead of 2025 — see Section 01. Pre-2025 Southwind history was earned on green complexes that no longer exist. This week's workbook weighted 2025-onward Southwind starts above career track record.
