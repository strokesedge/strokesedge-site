BMWChampionship.

Bellerive Country Club · Par 70 · 7,448 yards · Aug 20–23, 2026 · No-cut, 50-player field

The second leg of the FedEx Cup Playoffs lands at Bellerive Country Club, a Robert Trent Jones Sr. design built on his own "hard par, easy bogey" philosophy. At 7,448 yards for a par 70, this is one of the longest tests relative to par the Tour plays all year. Approach precision and off-the-tee production dominate the model this week, and the top of the FedEx Cup standings — Scottie Scheffler with a lead of more than 1,000 points after St. Jude — carries directly into how the market is pricing this field.

01 — Course Overview

Bellerive fingerprint.

Bellerive Country Club was designed by Robert Trent Jones Sr. and opened at its current Town and Country, Missouri site in 1959. It has hosted three prior majors and a professional Playoffs event — the 1965 U.S. Open (Gary Player, career Grand Slam), the 1992 PGA Championship (Nick Price), the 2018 PGA Championship (Brooks Koepka, holding off a closing 64 from Tiger Woods), and the 2008 BMW Championship (Camilo Villegas) — making this week the second time the Playoffs have visited this exact course.

What stats matter this week: SG: Approach is the model's top factor this week at 21%, with SG: Off the Tee close behind at 15%. At nearly 7,450 yards, there's no bunting around this course — driving distance is a structural requirement, not a bonus. Full numeric weights below.

#1 — SG: Approach (21% weight)
Long approach shots into big greens make overall approach quality the single biggest separator this week. Raised from an initial 18% proposal specifically to absorb weight trimmed from Putting and GIR%.
#2 — SG: Off the Tee (15% weight)
Extreme yardage for a par 70 rewards elite off-the-tee performance to shorten the course and set up scoring angles into Bellerive's large, well-guarded greens.
Prox 150-200yd Fairway (8% specialty weight)
The course's length pushes a disproportionate share of approach shots into the 150-200yd band specifically — this band isolates the specific proximity skill Bellerive's yardage actually tests.
Driving Accuracy Fit — only 4%
Tree-lined corridors do penalize wildness, but the model weights raw distance and approach precision far higher than fairway-hit accuracy this week. A short, accurate driver can't overcome 7,448 yards the way a long, slightly wilder one can.
03 — Course History

Who has been here before.

Bellerive's competitive sample is thin by design — one Tour stroke-play event this century (the 2008 BMW Championship, won by Camilo Villegas) plus the 2018 PGA Championship. The model applies just 3% weight each to Course History and Course Experience as a result.

Winning scores at Bellerive have swung from -6 (1992 PGA Championship) to -16 (2018 PGA Championship) to -15 (2008 BMW Championship), reflecting setup and conditions more than a stable course "par."

2018 PGA Championship — the most relevant comp
Brooks Koepka's 264 (-16) held off a final-round 64 from Tiger Woods (his best major final round), who finished 2nd at -14. Both players profiled as elite tee-to-green ball-strikers that week — the same combination (SG: Approach + SG: Off the Tee) this week's model weights highest.
