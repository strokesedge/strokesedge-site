Bank of Utah Championship 2026 Recap: Smotherman Wins at +10000 and the Model Picks Graded

Austin Smotherman, our +10000 longshot at model rank #15, won his first PGA Tour title in his 100th start. Every pick graded.

Published Oct 6, 2026

Excuse my french, but we F****** DID IT!!

Austin Smotherman won the 2026 Bank of Utah Championship, and he did it from the longshot slot on our card.

Anyone following hopefully won at least $500 (I’m praying our followers are throwing at least $5 on these longshots) At +10,000 and model rank #15, Smotherman was the StrokesEdge LONGSHOT / VALUE pick in the published preview. How Smotherman closed it out

He opened 63-62-65 and carried a one-shot lead over Ghim into Sunday. Then the 13th happened. A double bogey at the par-4, where his approach plugged in a bunker, tightened things, and Trace Crowe’s 40-foot eagle at 14 cut the margin to one.

One shot. That was the whole cushion.

Smotherman answered with a 293-yard drive onto the 14th green and a birdie, then closed with a 68 to finish at 258.

How the model’s picks played

Longshot / Value: Smotherman won

Austin Smotherman (+10000, model rank #15) won outright. The preview flagged matching 87th percentile marks in approach and driving and called him a ball-striking-first play, with putting at the 58th percentile as the only middling number. A 63-62 start at a birdie-heavy course with generous fairways is what that profile looks like when it clicks.

The other longshot, Seamus Power (+8000, rank #12), finished T21 at -17. His 90th percentile putting was real, but his 37th percentile driving, the weakness the preview named, kept him outside the top 20.

E/W Winner: Keefer and Kohles live, Greyserman misses

Johnny Keefer (+3500, rank #3) finished T10 at -19, the best result of the three. His 97th percentile putting and 92nd percentile approach delivered a top-10 without ever threatening Smotherman. Ben Kohles (+4500, rank #5) finished T13 at -18, inside the top 20 but not close to the lead.

Max Greyserman (+4500, rank #7) missed the cut at -5. The preview called him a one-dimensional putting bet with a 56th percentile approach game, and the approach game is what failed him.

Top 10 / Top 20: the weakest tier

Ben James (rank #2) finished T5 and Doug Ghim (rank #4) finished second, so both cashed in the finish markets. The other four did not. Jackson Koivun (rank #1) finished T65 at -8, Kevin Yu (rank #8) finished T32, Zecheng Dou (rank #9) finished T67, and Maverick McNealy (rank #6) missed the cut. That is 2 of 6.

The model’s top-ranked player, with a 78.9 L1 score and a 97th percentile approach number, never got into contention. The preview’s thesis of trusting the ball-striking profile did not hold for Koivun this week.

Fades: all three held

Michael Brennan (+1600) finished T13, Ben James (+1500) finished T5, and Jackson Koivun (+1200) finished T65. None won. Each fade was outright only, with Top 10 and Top 20 listed as still viable.

James is the clearest example of the split working as written: fade the outright, play the finish market. Brennan’s T13 would have been a fine finish-market play too. Koivun’s finish-market viability did not pan out.

Matchups: 1-1

Keefer over Yu was a win (T10 versus T32). Greyserman over McCarty was a loss, with Greyserman missing the cut and McCarty finishing T53 at -11.

Overall, the model had both the winner and the runner-up on the published card, plus two other top-10 finishers in James and Keefer, and T13s from Kohles and Brennan. The misses were concentrated in the finish-market tier and in the top two preview names, Koivun and McNealy.

What the model got right and what it did not

The Smotherman call is the one that matters for how this model is meant to work. His rank #15 sat well below the headline names, but approach and driving were the two highest-weighted categories in the framework, and his putting was merely average, not a liability. Ghim (rank #4) and James (rank #2) finishing second and T5 fits the same tee-to-green-first approach.

The clearest miss is Koivun. The preview framed him as a quality signal, not a value bet, and his edge was already negative at -0.68%. Even so, a #1 rank that finishes T65 is a miss on the model’s highest-confidence slot. Black Desert’s thin course history and the altitude adjustment may be leaving something out for young, first-time visitors. Worth tracking, nothing more yet.
