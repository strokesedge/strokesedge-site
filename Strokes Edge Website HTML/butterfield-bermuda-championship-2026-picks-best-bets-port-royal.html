Butterfield Bermuda Championship 2026 Picks: Model Best Bets and Fades for Port Royal Golf Course

Approach and putting play lead the model at Port Royal. Best bets, fades and a +3300 value play for the 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship.

Published Oct 21, 2026 · Port Royal Golf Course · Southampton, Bermuda · 2026-10-22 · Par 71 · 6,828 Yds

The 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship runs 2026-10-22 at Port Royal Golf Course, Southampton, Bermuda.

Port Royal is a short, exposed course on the Bermuda coast. Nothing here gets decided by distance, so the model leans on iron play and the putter, and the wind forecast gives it more to chew on than usual.

Model Framework

  • SG: Approach, 22%. On a 6,828-yard par 71 most approaches are short and mid-iron shots, so approach quality is the main separator.
  • SG: Putting, 14%. Scoring on short courses is putt-driven, and a large share of the leaderboard is decided on the greens.
  • SG: Around the Green, 10%. Wind and firm coastal turf leave plenty of missed greens, so around-the-green skill protects scores.
  • Course-Fit Approach Component, 9%. Data Golf's course-fit approach component captures player-specific fit to this shorter approach profile.
  • SG: Off the Tee, 8%. Driving matters mainly for fairway position in the wind, not raw length, so it gets modest weight.
  • Driving Accuracy Fit, 8%. Wind and tight tree-lined corridors reward keeping the ball in play more than distance.
  • Proximity 100-150yd Fairway, SG-per-shot, 7%. Short course means a high share of approaches from 100 to 150 yards, so proximity from that band matters.
  • Course-Fit Short-Game Component, 6%. Short-game fit adds signal beyond the generic around-the-green number on small, contoured greens.
  • Scrambling %, up-and-down rate from missed greens, 5%. Wind pushes more approaches off the greens, so up-and-down rate carries real value.
  • Greens in Regulation %, 3%. Greens hit are a direct proxy for ball-striking success when the course does not demand length.
  • Birdie or Better %, 3%. A scoreable par 71 means birdie volume separates the field.
  • Driving Distance Fit, 2%. Length is a small edge at 6,828 yards, so distance is nearly neutral here.
  • Course History, 2%. Past results here carry some information but the sample is thin for much of the field.
  • Course Experience, 1%. Prior reps on the layout help slightly with reading wind and green speeds.

Winner DNA gates this week:

  • SG: Approach >= -0.1. Approach is the lead stat at a short course, so plausible winners should not be clearly below-average iron players.
  • SG: Putting >= -0.15. A strongly negative putter rarely wins a short, putt-driven event.
  • Driving Accuracy Fit >= -0.4. Wind makes severe driving inaccuracy too costly for a contender.

Weather

Wind running 14-43 mph across the tournament window, gusts up to 60 mph. Temperatures 72-78F. 4 day(s) in the window carrying meaningful rain.

The forecast window shows winds of 14 to 43 mph with gusts to 60 and four days of meaningful rain, which is a lot for a course with little protection from the Atlantic. Wind at that level punishes loose contact and rewards players who control trajectory, so we lean harder on approach and short-game numbers. Forecasts at that range can move. Check conditions before locking in prices, because a softer week would make birdies cheaper and favor putting variance.

Course History Note

Port Royal has hosted this event every year since 2019, and winning scores have swung with the weather, from Brendon Todd and Camilo Villegas at 24-under to Adam Schenk at 12-under last year. Seven editions is a thin sample next to a standard tour stop, which is why course history carries only 2% in the model this week. Many in the field have little or no tour experience on this layout, so the market is partly guessing, and that is where the edge shows up.
