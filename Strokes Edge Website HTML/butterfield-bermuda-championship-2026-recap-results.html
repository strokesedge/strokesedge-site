Butterfield Bermuda Championship 2026 Results: The Model's #1 Pick Smotherman Wins at +3300

The model's #1 player won at +3300. The call, the full scorecard, and where to get the model.

Published Oct 28, 2026

The model’s #1 player just won the 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship. Austin Smotherman beat Seamus Power on the third hole of a Monday playoff at Port Royal Golf Course. He was the best-edge play in our picks article at +3300, with a 4.41% edge, and he went out and won it.

A second PGA Tour win in less than a month. A $1,080,000 check. A final round that wind canceled before anyone teed off. Below: the call we made, the full scorecard (wins and misses, because that is what a public model is for), and how to get the model for yourself.

The Call We Made

The model had Smotherman at 7.36% to win. The price at +3300 implied about 2.9%. That 4.41 point gap was the biggest edge on the entire card, and it belonged to the player who won.

That is the product in one picture: find the player the price undersells, put it in writing, and let the record show what happens. Three things went the model’s way on the card:

  • Model rank #1 won the tournament.

  • Seamus Power over Kevin Yu won. Power finished solo second.

  • The outright fade on Ben James held. He missed the cut.

One 7% shot landing does not prove a model. A public record over a full season does, and that is what the pick tracker is for. We do not have this week’s strokes gained data yet, so we cannot say whether the iron play that graded in the 96th percentile is what won it. We will check.

The Result

Sunday’s final round never started. The Tour delayed tee times for gusts over 40 mph, then canceled play that evening, and declared the results final through 54 holes. That left Austin Smotherman (65-66-66) and Seamus Power (66-64-67) tied at 16 under, two clear of Taylor Moore, Corey Conners and Ben Silverman at 14 under.

The playoff went off Monday morning on the par-3 16th, sudden death. Both players parred it twice. On the third extra hole Smotherman made par, Power missed the green and made bogey, and that was the tournament. It was only the second playoff in the event’s history, and the first since Brian Gay beat Wyndham Clark in 2020.

Smotherman takes home $1,080,000 and a second PGA Tour win in less than a month, after the Bank of Utah Championship three weeks earlier. Power collects $654,000 and misses out on becoming the first two-time champion at Port Royal, so the event still has no repeat winner.

One small thing from the archive. Smotherman shared the first-round lead here in 2022 with a 62. Our model gave course history 2% of the weight this week, and one data point is not a reason to change that.

The Full Scorecard

Here is the full scorecard, because a model only earns trust if it shows everything. Smotherman’s win is the headline. This is the rest of the top 11.

Past the winner, the card was uneven. Of the model’s top 11 players, eight made the cut and three did not: Ben James (#3), Kevin Streelman (#4) and Patton Kizzire (#6). Only Smotherman finished in the top 10. Four finished in the top 20: Smotherman, Luke Clanton (T11), Ben Kohles (T17) and Adrien Dumont De Chassart (T17).

Trace Crowe, rank #2, had the best SG: Approach number in the field going in (+1.279) and finished T34 at 8 under. Streelman (73-68) and Kizzire (70-70) both missed the cut. James (70-71) missed it by four strokes at 1 under, against a cut line of 5 under.

Eleven players is a small sample for judging a model, in either direction. It is also all the evidence this week gives us.

Matchups

One win, two losses, scored on the official 54-hole results. Check how your own book settled a shortened event, since rules differ.

  • Seamus Power over Kevin Yu: won. Power finished at 16 under and lost the playoff, Yu (74-70) missed the cut. This was the thinnest L1 gap of the three, 57.0 to 55.4, which we called a lean.

  • Kevin Roy over Eric Cole: lost. Cole opened 65-65, then shot 74 in round three and still finished at 9 under. Roy (69-68-73) finished at 3 under.

  • Max McGreevy over Davis Thompson: lost by one stroke. McGreevy finished at 7 under, Thompson at 8 under. This was the widest L1 gap on the card, 59.8 to 48.4, and it lost by one stroke.

The widest gap lost and the narrowest won. Three matchups tell you nothing about whether L1 gaps predict results, so we are not going to pretend otherwise.

Where We Missed

Ben James. The outright fade held up, since he missed the cut. The Top 10 and Top 20 note we attached to it did not. We rated him #3 on the board, called the problem his price, and he finished 4 strokes outside the cut line. We would rather say that than count the fade as a clean win.

Kevin Streelman (+8000) and Patton Kizzire (+25000). Both carried positive edges and both missed the cut. Streelman was a putter play (96th percentile) and went 73-68. Kizzire went 70-70. The longshot note in the picks article said to keep these small, and this is the reason for that note.

The passes. We passed on Ben Kohles (+3300, -0.26% edge), Zac Blair (+3500, -0.61%) and Max McGreevy (+4000). Kohles finished T17, Blair T25, McGreevy T44. Those passes were about price, not about whether the players would have a good week, and Kohles finishing inside the top 20 is the one that stings a little.

A single week does not change the weights. What we are checking when strokes gained data posts is whether Smotherman’s flat around-the-green number mattered in a windy week, and how the two putting-led plays, Streelman and Ewart, got their results.

The Forecast

We flagged winds of 14 to 43 mph, gusts up to 60 mph and four days of rain, and said forecasts at that range move. The part that mattered was Sunday. The Tour delayed tee times for gusts over 40 mph, then canceled the round. We only have the Tour’s “over 40 mph” figure, so we cannot grade the 60 mph number.

Two things follow. First, a 54-hole score is not comparable to the 72-hole winning scores in the history chart from our picks article, which ran from 12 under to 24 under. Smotherman’s 16 under stays off that chart. Second, a canceled round changes how finish and matchup markets settle at some books, so check yours before assuming a result.
