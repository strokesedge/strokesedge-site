Course Guide · 2026 Bank of Utah Championship

BlackDesert.

Ivins, Utah · Par 71 · 7,421 tournament yards · Host: Bank of Utah Championship (Oct 1-4)

Black Desert Resort hosts its third straight PGA Tour event, and the numbers say the same thing every year: this course gets torched. Matt McCarty shot 23-under to win the 2024 inaugural, Michael Brennan went 22-under to defend the title in 2025, and Data Golf has it ranked as the ninth-easiest course on Tour across its first two years. Wide bentgrass fairways, 7,000-square-foot greens, and thin desert air at 3,000 feet make this a birdie fest, until an errant swing finds the exposed lava rock lining nearly every hole.

01 — Course Fingerprint

Tom Weiskopf's desert birdie machine.

Black Desert Resort opened in 2023, designed by Tom Weiskopf with Phil Smith, cut through an ancient lava field and framed by red sandstone cliffs in Ivins, Utah, just outside St. George. The par-71 layout runs an unusual hole distribution for a Tour venue: 11 par 4s, four par 3s, and only three par 5s, plus a routing that sits around 3,000 to 3,100 feet of elevation, which means the ball travels noticeably farther than sea-level numbers suggest in the thin desert air.

Fairways, tees, and greens run creeping bentgrass; the rough is Kentucky bluegrass, kept short enough that it rarely punishes a miss on its own. The real hazard is architectural: greens average roughly 7,000 square feet and fairways are wide, but drives that miss the short grass can bound into exposed black lava rock lining the corridors, an unplayable, ball-swallowing hazard that has nothing to do with a normal rough or bunker miss. The model treats Black Desert as an approach-and-putting course first, with driving accuracy mattering more for hazard avoidance than for scoring, since a manageable rough and flat green complexes otherwise reward volume golf.

Wind is the one true equalizer
Black Desert can see gusts up to 40 mph off the surrounding terrain, and it's the biggest swing factor on an otherwise gettable golf course. A calm morning wave can post a number that a windy afternoon wave can't touch, which matters for in-tournament live markets more than most weeks on Tour.

03 — Two Years of Results

Low scores, first-time winners.

Black Desert's short Tour history already has a clear pattern: low winning scores and new names hoisting the trophy. Matt McCarty won the inaugural 2024 event at 23-under (261), still the course record, by three strokes. Michael Brennan followed in 2025 at 22-under (262), a four-shot win built on four rounds of 67 or better, and returns in 2026 as the defending champion.

First-time winners, not a coincidence
Two editions, two players winning for the first time on Tour. A gettable setup with low variance off the tee lets a hot week translate directly into a trophy, which is exactly the kind of course where a form-driven, currently-streaking player can outrun a bigger name who isn't seeing the ball well that week.
04 — Betting Angles

How to bet a birdie-fest with teeth.

01
Prioritize hot approach and putting form over pure length
With winning scores of -22 and -23 in the first two editions, this is a week to weight current-form SG: Approach and SG: Putting over raw driving distance, which matters less at an altitude-shortened track with forgiving rough.
02
Respect the lava rock in accuracy props
Wide fairways make this look like a bomber's paradise, but a stray drive into exposed lava rock is an automatic penalty stroke with no recovery shot, unlike a normal rough miss. Players who avoid the rock entirely tend to post the cleaner scorecards.
03
Watch the wind forecast before locking in bets
Gusts up to 40 mph are the one real defense this course has. A windy draw can add two or three strokes to a wave's average score relative to a calm one, so check tee-time wind splits before betting into a specific pairing or live market.
04
Don't overweight thin course history
Two editions is not enough sample to build a real "horses for courses" case. Both winners were first-time Tour champions riding hot form into the week, which argues for weighting current form over past finishes here specifically.
