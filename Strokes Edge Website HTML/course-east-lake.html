2026 Course Update — Material Change
East Lake underwent a 2023-24 Andrew Green restoration, unveiled at the 2024 TOUR Championship, that reshaped greens, bunkering, and strategic angles across the property, drawing on historical research (including a 1949 aerial photograph) to move the course back toward its Donald Ross-era design. Two hole assignments changed in the process. Greens were also regrassed to a heat-tolerant Bermuda strain, replacing the older bentgrass surfaces that struggled through Atlanta summers. This is functionally a different putting surface than the course most of the field's "East Lake experience" was built on — the model discounts Course History and Course Experience accordingly (3% each this week, down from a typical 5-6% combined) and leans harder on current-season strokes gained instead.
01 — Course Fingerprint

What East Lake actually demands.

East Lake Golf Club dates to 1904, making it the oldest golf course in Atlanta. Tom Bendelow laid out a second course on the property in 1908, Donald Ross redesigned it in 1913, George Cobb reworked it in 1959 ahead of the 1963 Ryder Cup, and Rees Jones restored it in the early 1990s after a period of neglect. Andrew Green's 2023-24 restoration is the latest and most significant chapter — see the course update above. It has been the permanent home of the TOUR Championship, the PGA Tour's season finale, since 2005.

The layout carries only two par 5s (holes 6 and 18) against four par 3s and twelve par 4s — an unusually par-4-heavy composition that leaves fewer traditional scoring opportunities than most Tour stops. Width off the tee is not the defense here. Fairways were widened in the restoration, and the model's own weighting reflects it: Driving Accuracy Fit carries just 4% this week, one of the lightest accuracy weights on the current schedule. What replaced it is precision — hitting the correct quadrant of greens that were rebuilt with sharper, more pronounced contours than the course carried before 2023.

The 30-man, no-cut field is also its own kind of filter. Every player already survived the FedEx Cup Playoffs to get here, so quality compression is extreme. The model's edge doesn't come from separating good players from bad ones — it comes from separating approach specialists from everyone else in a field where nearly everybody can drive it.

Model classification
East Lake is an approach-and-putting course with a driving-distance floor, not a driving-accuracy course. The player profile that wins: elite iron play into recontoured Bermuda greens, enough length to reach a stretched 7,440-yard layout in position, and a putting stroke that can hold up on greens still building a competitive track record. Short, accurate players without approach precision are disadvantaged. So are bombers who can't find greens — width off the tee only matters if the next shot finds the putting surface.
02 — Predictive Stats

What the data says actually matters.

StrokesEdge's Data Golf-sourced L1 weights for the 2026 TOUR Championship, blended 60% skill-ratings baseline / 40% computed L30 form. Approach carries the top weight by a clear margin, with off-the-tee and putting rounding out the three categories that combine for 44% of the model.

SG: Approach
#1 Weight — 17%
Long approach shots into rebuilt, more contoured greens make approach quality the top differentiator this week. The Winner DNA gate requires SG: Approach ≥ +0.05 — no plausible winner profile clears East Lake's small greens without demonstrated current iron form.
SG: Off the Tee
#2 Weight — 14%
7,440 yards demands strong tee-to-green ball-striking just to reach par-4 and par-5 greens in scoring position. This is about getting into the fairway with a full swing, not steering it — see the Driving Accuracy note below.
SG: Putting
#3 Weight — 13%
Renovated Bermuda greens with new contours reward elite putters disproportionately. Putting carries meaningfully more weight here than at most Tour stops — the market tends to treat East Lake as a ball-striker's course exclusively, and underrates the new greens as their own skill test.
Driving Distance Fit
#4 Weight — 8%
Elite length helps attack a stretched-out, par-70 layout with reachable par 5s. Data Golf's course-fit distance component, not raw driving distance rank — it isolates the specific benefit of length at this exact routing.
SG: Around the Green
#5 Weight — 7%
Firmer, faster surfaces post-restoration still punish short-game misses around slick greens, even with the driving-accuracy premium reduced. A miss still has to be recovered.
Driving Accuracy Fit
Low weight — 4% only
Widened fairways from the 2023-24 redesign reduce the accuracy premium relative to older East Lake setups. The market's instinct to reward "tidy" ball-strikers at East Lake is weaker evidence than it looks — the course simply doesn't punish a wayward tee shot the way it used to, as long as the approach that follows finds the green.

The accuracy trap
The most common market mistake at East Lake right now is pricing East Lake "veterans" and steady, accurate ball-strikers as automatic fits, on the strength of a reputation the course earned before 2023. The restoration widened fairways and rebuilt the greens — the accuracy premium that used to matter here is gone, and the putting/approach premium on the new surfaces hasn't been fully priced in yet. The model fades outright value on players whose case rests mainly on "East Lake experience" rather than current approach and putting form.
04 — Key Holes

Where the par 70 is won.

Only two par 5s on the entire card, four par 3s (including one of the longest on Tour), and a back nine that closes with a genuine risk-reward finishing hole. Yardages reflect the 2026 pgatour.com course card, post-restoration.

05 — Historical Trends

What recent FedEx Cup finales tell us.

East Lake has produced a run of approach-first champions — Cantlay in 2021, Hovland in 2023, Scheffler in 2024 all ranked near the top of the field in strokes gained on approach that week. Scores below reflect the pre-2026 staggered-start scoring format, where players began the week with bonus strokes tied to their FedEx Cup seed — not literal stroke-play totals. See the format note below.

YearWinnerScoreKey model signal
2025 Tommy Fleetwood -18 (262) First PGA Tour win, in his 164th start. Won by three over Patrick Cantlay and Russell Henley. Consistent tee-to-green week under the reduced-staggered-start format.
2024 Scottie Scheffler -30 Held off Collin Morikawa for his first FedEx Cup. Elite approach and off-the-tee numbers all week — the prototypical model profile before this year's format change.
2023 Viktor Hovland -27 Won by five over Xander Schauffele with a final-round 63. Approach-driven performance, third-youngest FedEx Cup champion ever.
2022 Rory McIlroy -21 Third FedEx Cup title, a comeback win on Sunday. McIlroy is the only three-time East Lake winner (2016, 2019, 2022).
2021 Patrick Cantlay -21 Approach game carried the week — a template result the model still leans on for how East Lake rewards iron precision over raw length.
2020 Dustin Johnson -21 Complete ball-striking week, driving distance and approach both elite — the closest historical comp to a distance-plus-approach profile the current model still rewards.
Format note — 2026 is genuinely different
Every year above used a staggered start, where the FedEx Cup points leader began the TOUR Championship at a stroke or more under par before a shot was hit. 2026 removes that entirely — every player in the 30-man field starts the week at even par, straight stroke play, with the tournament winner also becoming the FedEx Cup champion outright. That makes the historical scores above useful for player-profile signal, but not directly comparable as raw numbers to whatever wins in 2026.
06 — Betting Angles

How to bet East Lake smart.

01
Approach is the whole ballgame this year
At 17% of the model — the single largest weight on the current schedule — SG: Approach is doing more work at East Lake in 2026 than at almost any other course StrokesEdge has covered. The restoration's new green contours reward it directly. If a player's case doesn't start with current-season approach form, the case is weak regardless of pedigree.
02
Don't pay the accuracy premium
Driving Accuracy Fit carries just 4% weight this week. Wider fairways post-restoration mean the market's instinct to reward "tidy" East Lake specialists is stale evidence. Distance and approach quality matter far more than keeping the ball in the fairway by a wide margin.
03
Putting deserves more weight than the market gives it
New Bermuda greens with sharper contours put real weight on the putting stroke — 13% in the model, more than most Tour stops. The public narrative around East Lake is almost entirely ball-striking. The greens themselves are a live, still-developing skill test.
04
Discount "East Lake experience" claims
Course History and Course Experience are both down to 3% this week, about half their typical weight, because most of the field's East Lake reps happened on a course that no longer exists in its 2023-24 form. A player's TOUR Championship past is weaker evidence than usual — current form is the signal.
05
30-man, no-cut field compresses the longshot range
Every player already survived the FedEx Cup Playoffs to get here. Quality is extremely compressed relative to a full-field event, which limits realistic longshot upside. The model sizes value plays conservatively and leans more heavily on Top 10/Top 20 finish markets for the core of the card.
