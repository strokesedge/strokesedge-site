01 — Course Fingerprint

What Renaissance Club actually demands.

Renaissance Club in North Berwick, Scotland is a classic links course that hosts the Scottish Open, one of the PGA Tour's most prestigious events. At 7,266 yards with wide fairways and minimal rough, the course does not penalize tee shots the way parkland venues do. Instead, it punishes poor approach play with firm, fast-running greens and inconsistent lies from the ground.

Wind is the primary defense. Scottish coastal conditions mean wind direction and speed change the effective length and playability of every hole. The same par 4 plays radically different into the wind versus with the wind. The model classifies Renaissance Club as an accuracy-and-composure course where links expertise and weather adaptation matter more than pure distance.

Recent winners have been players who thrive in European links conditions—those with ryder Cup experience, prior wins on the European Tour, or demonstrated excellence at windswept courses. American players with limited links experience face a structural disadvantage despite comparable stats to European competitors.

Model classification
Renaissance Club is a links accuracy course with strong European Tour correlates. The profile that wins: strong course management and decision-making under variable conditions, elite SG: Approach into firm greens, prior links or European venue success, and experience managing wind-driven misses. Power hitters without links pedigree are downgraded. European Tour veterans are upgraded regardless of recent PGA Tour positioning.
