01 — Course Fingerprint

What Sedgefield actually demands.

Sedgefield is a Donald Ross design that underwent a $3 million, ten-month restoration in 2007 led by Ross restoration specialist Kris Spence, returning the course to its original routing and lengthening it for the modern Tour player. No further structural work has been made since: no green rebuilds, no tee relocations, no routing changes. This year's setup is the same course the field has played for most of the last two decades, just with the usual small, incremental yardage adjustments (7,131 yards in 2026, up from 7,030 as recently as 2023).

There's a quirk worth knowing before anything else: Sedgefield's members play the course to par 71. The 18th, a 507-yard closer that tumbles 50 feet downhill off the tee before climbing back up to a steeply bunkered green, is a par 5 on the member scorecard and a par 4 for the Wyndham Championship. That's where tournament par 70 comes from. It isn't a different hole count or a shorter course, just one hole reclassified for the pros.

The bigger structural fact is fairway width. Sedgefield plays across only 25 acres of short grass, tight by Tour standards, lined with mature trees and thick Bermuda rough. Combine that with a run of eight short-to-mid par 4s between 400 and 450 yards, and the course produces an approach-shot distribution unlike almost anywhere else on Tour: nearly 30% of approaches come from 150-175 yards, well above the Tour average. The model classifies Sedgefield as a positional course where the tee shot, not the driver, does the separating.

2026 course status
No material change from last year's event. The last structural work at Sedgefield was the 2007 Kris Spence restoration; everything since has been routine tee and yardage maintenance. Course history and prior Sedgefield form carry full predictive weight this year, nothing about the 2026 setup should discount it.
02 — Predictive Stats

What the data says actually matters.

Ranked by how consistently each category has separated the field at Sedgefield, based on the course's known approach-shot distribution and recent winner profiles.

Driving Accuracy
#1 Predictor
With only 25 acres of fairway and thick Bermuda rough throughout, missing the short grass at Sedgefield is costly in a way it isn't at wider, more forgiving Tour venues. The field hits well above 80% of greens in regulation from the fairway and barely over 50% from the rough, a bigger accuracy penalty than most weeks on Tour.
SG: Approach (50-100y wedge range)
#2 Predictor
The concentration of short-to-mid par 4s pushes nearly 30% of the field's approach shots into the 150-175 yard window, well above Tour average, and wedge proximity from 50-100 yards is one of the most cited predictors of success here. This is a shorter-iron and wedge test more than a long-iron test.
Bermuda Putting
#3 Predictor
Sedgefield's greens run small, averaging around 6,000 square feet, well below Tour average, on Bermuda grass. Players with a track record on Bermuda greens and at comparably tight, positional courses have historically had an edge here that pure ball-striking numbers alone don't capture.
Comp-Course Track Record
#4 Predictor
The same type of player wins the Wyndham Championship most years: an accurate ball-striker with prior results at Sedgefield itself or at similarly tight, tree-lined, positional venues. Multi-year Sedgefield form is a real signal, not noise, because the course hasn't structurally changed since 2007.
Driving Distance
Low predictor, not the separator
At 7,131 yards with a tight, tree-lined fairway grid, Sedgefield doesn't reward length the way a wide-open bomber's course does. The recent winner profile is accurate ball-strikers, not long hitters. Distance rank has not been the story at this event.

06 — Betting Angles

How to bet Sedgefield smart.

01
Accuracy is the gate here, not a bonus stat
At most Tour venues driving accuracy is a tiebreaker. At Sedgefield, with 25 acres of fairway and heavy Bermuda rough, it functions closer to a prerequisite. Screen for above-average accuracy before anything else.
02
Check wedge proximity specifically, not just overall approach rank
Because Sedgefield's short par-4 cluster pushes so many approaches into the 150-175 yard band, a player's overall SG: Approach rank can understate or overstate their actual Sedgefield fit. Wedge proximity from 50-100 yards is the more targeted number.
03
Weight Sedgefield-specific history heavily
Because the course hasn't structurally changed since the 2007 restoration, multi-year Sedgefield form is unusually reliable compared to course history at venues that get renovated more often. A player with three quiet-but-solid Sedgefield starts is saying something real.
04
Factor in FedExCup bubble motivation
This is the last event before the field is cut to the top 70 for the playoffs, with the top 50 after the second playoff event locking in 2027 signature-event status. Players sitting near those lines have more than prize money riding on the week, a real, if hard-to-quantify, motivation signal.
05
Don't pay up for distance alone
The last five winners are accurate ball-strikers, not bombers, and the course's tight fairway grid explains why. A short-priced favorite whose main selling point is driving distance is fighting the course's actual identity.
