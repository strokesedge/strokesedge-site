Course Guide — FedEx St. Jude Championship

TPCSouthwind.

Memphis, Tennessee · Par 70 · 7,288 yards (post-renovation) · Hosts: FedEx St. Jude Championship

TPC Southwind opens the FedExCup Playoffs, and this is only the second tournament played on a materially different golf course. Every green was rebuilt ahead of 2025 and the layout picked up roughly 50 yards. What hasn't changed is the winner profile: the last several champions here have been elite iron players first, sometimes despite a shaky driver. Water touches 11 holes, the fairways rank among the Tour's narrowest, and once a player finds the short grass, close to two-thirds of approach shots come from inside 175 yards.

01 — Course Fingerprint

What Southwind actually demands.

TPC Southwind opened in 1988, designed by Ron Prichard with Tour pros Hubert Green and Fuzzy Zoeller consulting. It has hosted PGA Tour golf almost continuously since 1989, first as the St. Jude Classic, then a WGC event from 2019-2021, and since 2022 as the FedExCup Playoffs opener. Water is in play on 11 of the 18 holes, headlined by the par-3 11th, an island green modeled on the same idea as TPC Sawgrass's 17th. The fairways rank among the narrowest on Tour, and the rough is thick, tacky Bermuda that has consistently ranked the course inside the Tour's top six for both penalty from missed fairways and approach difficulty from the rough.

The layout rewards a specific kind of miss-avoidance. Find the fairway and the course gives a scoring chance back: close to two-thirds of approach shots from the short grass come from inside 175 yards, a short-iron-heavy distribution. Miss it, and Southwind's rough and 94 bunkers take over. The model classifies Southwind as an approach-dominant course with a narrow-fairway penalty gate — hitting the fairway isn't optional, but once a player does, precision iron play separates the field far more than raw distance.

2026 course status — material change, verify before trusting old history
TPC Southwind rebuilt and resurfaced all 18 greens ahead of the 2025 tournament — the first resurfacing since 2004 and the biggest overhaul since 1998 — and sodded the surrounds in a firmer Zeon Zoysia grass for better drainage. The course also picked up roughly 50 yards in the process, most documented on the par-4 5th (485 yards in 2024 to 529 yards in 2025). Pgatour.com's own current listing shows 7,288 yards for 2026; Wikipedia's course page still shows the stale pre-renovation figure of 7,244 yards and doesn't mention the renovation at all — treat Wikipedia as out of date here, not as a second confirming source. Practical effect for the model: 2026 is only the second tournament on this setup, so multi-year "course history" built on the old green complexes carries less signal than usual, and recent-form/current-year approach data should be weighted above career Southwind track record until a longer post-renovation sample exists.
02 — Predictive Stats

What the data says actually matters.

Ranked by how consistently each category shows up in Southwind's recent winner profiles and the course's known shot distribution.

SG: Approach
#1 Predictor
This is the clearest signal at TPC Southwind. Every winner since the event moved here in 2019 — Brooks Koepka, Justin Thomas, Abraham Ancer, Will Zalatoris, Lucas Glover, Hideki Matsuyama, Justin Rose — has shared elite iron play that week. Several won a week where their driving was the shakiest part of their game. Approach carries the tournament by itself when it's elite enough.
Scrambling / Approach From Rough
#2 Predictor
Southwind ranks inside the Tour's top six for both penalty from missed fairways and approach difficulty from the rough, on some of the narrowest fairways on Tour. Missing the fairway here is common even for accurate players, so the ability to salvage par or better from the rough matters more than at a wider course.
Short-Iron Proximity (inside 175y)
#3 Predictor
Close to two-thirds of approach shots hit from the fairway come from inside 175 yards. A player's general SG: Approach number blends every distance band; wedge and short-iron proximity specifically is the more targeted read on Southwind fit.
Course History (post-2025 setup only)
#4 Predictor — caveat below
Southwind has hosted this event continuously since 1989, and repeat course fit has mattered historically. But the greens were fully rebuilt for 2025 — see Section 01 — so pre-2025 Southwind history is a weaker signal than usual this year. Only 2025-onward starts here should carry full weight.
Driving Accuracy / Off the Tee
Overweighted by the market
Narrow fairways make accuracy look like it should be the top predictor, and the market prices it that way. The actual winner sample doesn't support it: recent champions have combined shaky driving weeks with elite approach play and still won. Accuracy matters for the field broadly, but it hasn't been the separator among contenders.

06 — Betting Angles

How to bet Southwind smart.

01
Screen for approach first, not accuracy first
The market tends to price Southwind as an accuracy course because of the narrow fairways. The seven-year winner sample says otherwise: elite SG: Approach has been the actual requirement, and more than one champion won with an inconsistent driver that week.
02
Weight scrambling and rough-approach stats, not just fairway percentage
Because the fairways are genuinely narrow, missing them happens even to accurate drivers. What separates the field is what happens next — Southwind ranks top six in approach difficulty from the rough, so scrambling data is more predictive here than the raw driving accuracy number.
03
Discount pre-2025 course history, don't ignore it
The full green rebuild means a player's Southwind track record from 2019-2024 was earned on a different golf course. It's not worthless — the layout and hazards haven't moved — but a player's 2025-onward starts here should carry noticeably more weight than earlier ones.
04
Factor in FedExCup stakes, not just the purse
This is the field-of-70 opener, with the top 50 after the second Playoffs event locking in 2027 signature-event status. Players near those lines have more than the $20M purse riding on the week — a real, if hard-to-quantify, motivation signal.
05
Don't overpay for a pure accuracy narrative
A short-priced favorite whose main selling point is driving accuracy without a matching approach game is being priced on the course's reputation, not its actual winner sample. Southwind has rewarded elite iron players over elite drivers every year since 2019.
07 — FAQ

Quick answers.

What is par and yardage at TPC Southwind for the 2026 FedEx St. Jude Championship?
Par 70 at 7,288 yards, per pgatour.com's current tournament listing. That figure reflects a 2024-2025 renovation that lengthened the course roughly 50 yards. Wikipedia's course page still shows the pre-renovation figure of 7,244 yards.
Has TPC Southwind changed recently?
Yes. All 18 greens were rebuilt and resurfaced ahead of the 2025 tournament, the first resurfacing since 2004, with firmer Zeon Zoysia sod added around the greens and roughly 50 yards added to the course. 2026 is only the second tournament on this setup.
When is the 2026 FedEx St. Jude Championship?
Thursday through Sunday, August 13-16, 2026, at TPC Southwind in Memphis, Tennessee.
Why does the FedEx St. Jude Championship matter on the PGA Tour calendar?
It's the first FedExCup Playoffs event, played by the top 70 in points with no cut. The $20 million purse is on top of Playoffs positioning that determines who advances to the BMW Championship and, eventually, 2027 signature-event status.
What stats predict a winner at TPC Southwind?
SG: Approach, by a clear margin. Every winner since the event moved here in 2019 has had an elite iron-play week, sometimes despite inconsistent driving. Driving accuracy and distance are secondary to approach quality.
