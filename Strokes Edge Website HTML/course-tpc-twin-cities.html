01 — Course Fingerprint

What TPC Twin Cities actually demands.

TPC Twin Cities opened in 2000 in Blaine, Minnesota, roughly 15 miles north of Minneapolis-St. Paul. Arnold Palmer designed the course with Minnesota native and PGA Tour veteran Tom Lehman consulting. Bentgrass runs tee to green, the rough is moderate rather than penal, and water comes into play across a large share of the layout — a real design signature at this venue.

The tournament setup plays as a par 71 at 7,431 yards, and it has produced some of the lowest winning scores of any regular PGA Tour stop. Every 3M Open champion since the 2019 debut has finished between -15 and -24. The model classifies TPC Twin Cities as a birdiefest venue where the winner is decided by who makes the most birdies, not who avoids the fewest bogeys.

The market's shorthand for this course is "bomber's paradise," and big hitters have won here — but the data doesn't support treating distance as a hard requirement. An average-length driver with an elite iron game is just as live as a long hitter, provided the approach numbers are real. The winner profile is broader than the nickname suggests.

Model classification
TPC Twin Cities is a low-scoring, approach-driven birdiefest where off-the-tee distance opens opportunities but does not gate entry. The player profile that wins: elite SG: Approach into moderate-defense greens, a strong Birdie-or-Better rate, and comfort making aggressive swings in a field that's going low all four days. Purely accurate, conservative players without elite iron numbers get outscored here regardless of how straight they drive it.
02 — Predictive Stats

What the data says actually matters.

Ranked by historical correlation with finishing position at TPC Twin Cities, based on 3M Open results from 2019 through 2025.

SG: Approach the Green
#1 Predictor
Every 3M Open winner since 2019 has ranked in the top 20 of the field in SG: Approach for the week. In a low-scoring, birdie-heavy event, hitting it close enough to convert is the clearest separator — more predictive here than at courses where scrambling or distance dominate.
SG: Off the Tee
#2 Predictor
Roughly tied with approach play in the model's weighting. Length opens up short-iron approaches and reachable par 5s, and several champions (Matthew Wolff, Cameron Champ) were among the longest hitters on Tour in their winning year. But it is a strong signal, not a gate — see below.
Birdie-or-Better % (L50 rounds)
#3 Predictor
With winning scores routinely between -15 and -24, the event is decided by who converts the most birdie chances, not who avoids bogeys. Players trending hot on birdie rate over their last 50 rounds carry real signal into a course this permissive on scoring.
SG: Ball-Striking (Composite)
#4 Predictor
The combination of OTT and APP matters more than either stat alone. Michael Thompson's 2020 win — without elite distance — is the clearest evidence that all-around ball-striking, not raw power, is the real floor requirement here.
Driving Accuracy
Low predictor — overweighted by market
Moderate rough and generous fairway widths mean precision alone does not create separation. The market often prices "accurate, in-position" players as course fits. The data says a birdiefest rewards aggressive iron play far more than avoiding the light rough on the sides of the fairway.

No player has won the 3M Open more than once through 2025 — an unusually open field year to year for a tournament with this much history. The model does not apply a course-history bonus weight here the way it does at venues with repeat champions.
The accuracy trap
The most common market mistake at TPC Twin Cities is pricing conservative, in-the-fairway players as course fits because the layout is "playable." In a field where the winning number is routinely -20 or lower, players without elite Approach and Birdie-or-Better numbers get outscored even when they're hitting every fairway. The model fades outright positions on accuracy-first players who don't clear the APP and birdie-rate thresholds.
04 — The Closing Stretch

Where the tournament gets decided.

Holes 17 and 18 form the defining closing stretch at TPC Twin Cities — a short, all-carry par 3 over water followed by a lake-lined par 5 that tempts players into going for the green in two. Both holes were purpose-built by Arnold Palmer to create tournament drama on Sunday.

Hole Name / Type Par / Yards Key betting signal
17 All-Water Par 3 Par 3 · Short iron One of Arnold Palmer's signature short par 3s — played entirely over water. Club selection in the Minnesota wind is unforgiving. A poor swing here in the final group has ended more than one 3M Open bid.
18 Lake-Lined Closer Par 5 · Reachable in two A lake guards the right side of the fairway and the front of the green on the second shot. Long hitters get a legitimate eagle look; anyone laying up needs a precise wedge to a green fronted by water. This hole alone has decided multiple 3M Open finishes.
05 — Historical Trends

What the tournament's short history tells us.

The 3M Open has only been played since 2019, but the pattern in winning profiles is already clear: elite approach play is the constant, distance is a bonus, and no one has repeated as champion yet.

YearWinnerScoreKey model signal
2025 Kurt Kitayama -23 Made the cut by a single stroke, then shot a closing 60-65 to win by one over Sam Stevens. One of the hottest 36-hole closing stretches on Tour that year.
2024 Jhonattan Vegas -17 One-stroke win over Max Greyserman. Veteran ball-striker profile — steady approach play across all four rounds rather than one big scoring surge.
2023 Lee Hodges -24 Tournament scoring record. Seven-stroke margin built on elite approach play and a hot putter — the largest winning margin in event history.
2022 Tony Finau -17 Three-stroke win over Emiliano Grillo and Sungjae Im. Elite ball-striking across the bag — a top-tier composite T2G week.
2021 Cameron Champ -15 Two-stroke win. One of the longest hitters on Tour at the time — the clearest distance-driven winner in event history.
2020 Michael Thompson -19 Beat Adam Long by two. Not a distance-first player — the strongest evidence that elite approach and ball-striking alone can win here.
2019 Matthew Wolff -21 Inaugural champion, one-stroke win, in his first PGA Tour start as a professional. Power off the tee paired with hot short-term form.
A genuinely open field
Seven different champions in seven playings is a real signal in itself. Unlike venues where a handful of repeat winners dominate the model's course-history weighting, TPC Twin Cities rewards whoever is playing their best golf that specific week. The model leans more heavily on current-season form and less on multi-year course fit here than at almost any other stop on the schedule.
06 — Betting Angles

How to bet TPC Twin Cities smart.

01
Approach play is the real gate, not distance
The "bomber's paradise" label oversells the distance requirement. Michael Thompson's 2020 win proves elite ball-striking without top-tier length is entirely viable. Prioritize SG: Approach and Birdie-or-Better rate before looking at driving distance leaderboards.
02
Expect volatility from a hot closing stretch
The last two champions (Kitayama, Hodges) won largely on the strength of one or two exceptional rounds rather than sustained week-long dominance. In-tournament and live markets can move fast on Saturday and Sunday when someone catches fire.
03
Don't overpay for a "course history" angle
No repeat winners through seven playings means past top finishes here carry less predictive weight than at venues with a clear repeat-champion pattern. Weight current form more heavily than past 3M Open results.
04
Full-field cut event widens the longshot range
Unlike Signature Events with no cut and a capped field, the 3M Open is a standard full-field event with a 36-hole cut. That supports a wider, more aggressive longshot allocation than the model uses at Quail Hollow or Doral.
05
Watch the 17th and 18th in your matchup and finish bets
The all-water par-3 17th and the lake-lined par-5 18th have both directly decided finishes. Players with strong recent scoring on risk-reward closing holes carry a small but real edge in Top 5/Top 10 markets.
