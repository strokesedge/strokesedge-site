How an each-way bet works

You place two equal bets on the same player: one on the player to win and one on the player to place. If the player wins, both bets pay. If the player places but does not win, only the place bet pays. If the player misses the places, you lose both stakes.

The win part pays the full odds. The place part pays a fraction of the odds, typically 1/4 or 1/5 in golf, and your place stake is returned. With decimal odds D, the win part returns stake x D and the place part returns stake x (1 + (D - 1) x fraction).

Worked example

A $5 each-way bet on a player at +2000 (decimal 21) with place terms of 1/4 odds costs $10. If the player wins, the win part returns $105 and the place part returns $30, for $135 total and $125 profit. If the player only places, you get $30 back for $20 profit. If the player misses the places, you lose the $10.

Each-way betting in the US

Each-way terms are standard at UK and Irish bookmakers. Most US sportsbooks do not list each-way golf bets the same way. They usually offer separate top 5, top 10 and top 20 markets instead, each priced on its own. This calculator is still useful for comparing an each-way bet with the same stake split across a win market and a finish market. See the PGA Tour betting guide and the golf odds calculator.

FAQ

What is an each-way bet in golf?

An each-way bet is two equal bets on one player: one to win and one to place. The place part pays a fraction of the odds, commonly 1/4 or 1/5, if the player finishes in the places the sportsbook sets.

How do you calculate each-way returns?

The win part returns stake times decimal odds. The place part returns stake times one plus the decimal odds minus one, multiplied by the place fraction. Add the two when the player wins.

Do US sportsbooks offer each-way golf bets?

Most do not offer them in the UK style. They usually list top 5, top 10 and top 20 finish markets as separate bets, each with its own price.
