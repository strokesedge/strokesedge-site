FedEx St. Jude Championship 2026 Recap: Scheffler Wins by 8, Finish Markets Carry the Week

TPC Southwind · Memphis, TN · August 14–17, 2026

Published Aug 18, 2026

Winner, winner! Another winning week for Strokesedge!

Scottie Scheffler shot 17-under 263 at TPC Southwind and won the 2026 FedEx St. Jude Championship by eight strokes. Si Woo Kim finished runner-up at -9. Scheffler posted a third-round 61 on Friday and the tournament was effectively over before the weekend started. Alex Noren and Sam Burns shared third at -8. It was the kind of week where one player made the entire outright market structurally wrong before Saturday tee times even went out.

What the Model Got Right

The model’s call on Scheffler was the defining analytical decision of the week. He graded as the #1 ranked player in the field, field-best L1 score, elite approach and off-the-tee numbers. A 2nd percentile course-fit approach component dragged his edge to -6.27%, producing a call of FADE OUTRIGHT ONLY, Top 10/20 still viable. No outright winner bet on Scheffler ever went on the tracker. The only exposure was a Scheffler/Fleetwood Top 10 two-leg parlay at +275, which cashed when both players finished T7. That’s the correct structural response to a week where the best player in the field is also a legitimate course-fit fade: build around him rather than on him.

Si Woo Kim at +3300 E/W Winner was the week’s best single outright call. The model had him #3 in the field, 88th percentile approach game, a real fit for Southwind’s green complex. He finished runner-up. The each-way ticket paid $46.25 on a $10 stake.

The Top 20 tier did exactly what the tier is built to do in a week where one player runs away with the outright market. Six of seven Top 20 tickets cashed. Tommy Fleetwood and Hideki Matsuyama, both model top-five plays, finished T7. Patrick Cantlay, Sam Burns, Viktor Hovland, and Tom Kim all finished inside the number. Burns also cashed a Group Winner ticket at +350. The structure held up even though the outright market was unwinnable.

Where the Model Missed

Eight outright winner bets went on the tracker. All eight lost. That’s not unusual when one player shoots 61 in round three and leads by seven before the weekend, but it’s still eight losing tickets, and it needs to be said plainly.

Robert MacIntyre was the week’s sharpest miss. Both his Winner and Top 20 tickets lost, the only model-backed play that couldn’t cash even the finish-market ticket. No explanation other than he just didn’t have his week.

Jackson Koivun’s E/W Winner ticket lost. He was logged as a Watch List name at the time of picks publication, course history note, insufficient Data Golf sample, not a model-backed play in the regression sense, and the result matched that framing. Still a loss on the tracker, still worth noting.

The outright tier as a whole was structurally difficult this week in a way the model’s numbers can’t fully account for in advance: when one player leads by seven on Saturday morning, any outright winner bet not on that player is essentially dead regardless of how strong the underlying model case was. The seven Top 20 tickets were the right exposure to carry into a week like that. The eight outright bets were the wrong one.

The Bigger Picture

A 9-10 week still closed at +$100.81 and a 57.5% return. That’s the finish-market structure working exactly as designed. The outright tier went 0-8 and the week was still profitable because the Top 20 tier covered it and then some, and because one E/W ticket on a real model conviction at +3300 paid $46 on a $10 stake. You don’t need a high hit rate on outright bets to run a positive week when the odds are sized correctly and the finish markets are doing their job.

Worth saying plainly: the model didn’t call Scheffler as the winner. It called him as a fade outright and a play on the finish market at the parlay number. That’s a meaningful distinction and the tracker reflects it accurately.

FedEx Cup Standings After St. Jude

Scheffler leads with 4,873 points, over 1,000 clear of Matt Fitzpatrick (3,425) in second. Cameron Young sits third at 3,182. Si Woo Kim jumped to fourth on the back of his runner-up finish.

The top 50 in points advance to the BMW Championship and that field is now locked. Sungjae Im moved 13 spots on his T5, sliding into the top 50 and pushing Keith Mitchell to 51st, just out of the field. Jordan Spieth, Max Homa, Shane Lowry, and Brian Harman were among the 20 players who didn’t advance. Rickie Fowler sits at 30th, the current cutline inside the top 50 for advancing again to the Tour Championship after BMW.
