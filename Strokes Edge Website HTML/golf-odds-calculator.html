How to convert golf odds

American odds show the profit on a $100 stake. A positive number such as +1325 means $1,325 profit on $100, so the decimal price is 1 + 1325 / 100 = 14.25. A negative number such as -110 means you stake $110 to win $100, so the decimal price is 1 + 100 / 110 = 1.91. Fractional odds like 53/4 show profit over stake, so the decimal price is 1 + 53 / 4 = 14.25.

How to find implied probability

Implied probability is 1 divided by the decimal odds. At 14.25 that is 1 / 14.25 = 7.0 percent. That is the chance the sportsbook needs you to believe, before its margin. In a golf outright market the implied probabilities of every player add up to more than 100 percent, and the extra is the book's margin.

What edge means

Edge is your estimated win probability minus the implied probability. If you think a player wins 9 percent of the time and the price implies 7.0 percent, the edge is 2.0 percentage points. Expected value per $100 is 100 x (your probability x decimal odds - 1). StrokesEdge uses the same idea each week, with a course-specific model supplying the probability. FAQ

How do you convert American odds to implied probability?

For positive odds, divide 100 by the odds plus 100. +1325 gives 100 / 1425, or 7.0 percent. For negative odds, divide the odds without the minus sign by that number plus 100. -110 gives 110 / 210, or 52.4 percent.

What does +1325 mean in golf betting?

It means a winning $100 bet profits $1,325 and returns $1,425 in total. The implied win probability is about 7.0 percent.

What is a good edge in golf betting?

Any positive edge means your probability is higher than the price implies, but small edges are easily erased by estimation error and the sportsbook margin. Treat the result as a way to check your reasoning, not a guarantee.

Why do the implied probabilities in a golf market add up to more than 100 percent?

The extra is the sportsbook's margin, sometimes called the vig or overround. It is built into every price in the market.
