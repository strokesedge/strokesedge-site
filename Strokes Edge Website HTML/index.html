How a pick gets made.
Step 1 · The course

Work out what the course asks.

Every venue asks different questions. We find which strokes gained categories have actually separated the field there before we look at a single player.

Step 2 · The field

Score every player against it.

Each player is rated on those demands and screened against minimum thresholds. A player who fails a threshold can still be a top 10 candidate, but not an outright bet.

Step 3 · The price

Compare the model to the market.

The model's win probability is set against the sportsbook price. If we like a player more than the market does, that gap is the edge. If there is no gap, there is no bet.

FAQ

PGA Tour betting, answered.

What is the best way to find value in PGA Tour betting?

Compare a player's strokes gained profile to what the course demands, then compare the model's win probability to the sportsbook price. A bet has value only when the model probability is higher than the price implies. StrokesEdge screens every player in the field this way each week.

Are the PGA Tour betting picks free?

Yes. The weekly picks article is free on the StrokesEdge Substack, and the top of every analysis page is free. The full model workbook is $7 per tournament, or included with the $21 monthly membership.

Which stats matter most for PGA Tour betting?

It changes by course. Strokes gained approach is the most common predictor, but the model re-weights approach, off the tee, around the green, putting and course fit for each venue.

Does StrokesEdge publish its results?

Yes. Every bet is logged on the public pick tracker with odds, wager and result, wins and losses included.
