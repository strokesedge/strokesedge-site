How PGA Tour odds work

Golf odds in the US are quoted in American format. A +2000 price pays $2,000 on a $100 stake, plus the stake back. The price also tells you the probability the sportsbook is implying: divide 100 by the odds plus 100.

OddsImplied probabilityWhat it means
+50016.7%A heavy favorite in a full field
+12007.7%A top-tier contender
+30003.2%A solid mid-range play
+100001.0%A true longshot

Those percentages are what the book needs you to believe. Betting is profitable only when you think the real chance is higher than that number.

The main betting markets

  • Outright winner. The player must win the tournament. Highest payout, highest variance.
  • Each-way. Half the stake goes on the win, half on a place. Place terms vary by book, so check them before you bet.
  • Top 5, top 10, top 20. The player must finish in that range. Prices are shorter, so hits are more frequent.
  • Make or miss the cut. A simple market on whether the player plays the weekend.
  • Head-to-head matchups. Two players, whoever finishes ahead wins. Ties are handled differently by each book.
  • Three-balls and first-round leader. Round-level markets that reward scoring-form research.

How to find value

Value means the price is longer than the player's true chance deserves. To estimate that chance you need a model, even a simple one. StrokesEdge builds a course-specific score from strokes gained, course-fit and form inputs, converts it to a win probability, and compares it to the market. The gap between the two is the edge.

Edge = model win probability minus market implied probability. A positive edge is where the model thinks the price is too long.

Why the course matters

A player's overall rating tells you how good they are. The course tells you which skills pay. A long course with firm greens leans on strokes gained approach and off the tee. A short, tight course can flip the weighting toward accuracy and short game. That is why StrokesEdge re-weights its model for every venue and publishes a course guide for each one.

Course changes matter too. When a course is renovated or rerouted, past results carry less weight. This week's World Wide Technology Championship analysis shows the weights in practice.

Staking and bankroll

Use a fixed unit, usually a small slice of your bankroll, and size every bet in units. Longshots get fewer units than shorter-priced finish markets. Never chase a losing week by raising stakes. Keep a record of every bet, including the losers, as the StrokesEdge pick tracker does.

Common mistakes

  • Betting only the favorites, whose prices already reflect their quality.
  • Ignoring place terms on each-way bets.
  • Betting before the field and weather are confirmed.
  • Using last season's course notes after a renovation.
  • Not shopping for the best price across sportsbooks.

Betting should be a hobby you can afford. If it stops being fun, take a break and use the help line in the footer.

FAQ

What are the main PGA Tour betting markets?

The main markets are outright winner, each-way, top 5, top 10 and top 20 finishes, make or miss the cut, head-to-head matchups, three-ball bets and first-round leader. Outright and finish-position markets are the most common.

How do you convert American odds to a probability?

For positive odds, divide 100 by the odds plus 100. A +2000 price implies 100 / 2100, or about 4.8 percent. For negative odds, divide the odds (without the minus sign) by that number plus 100.

Why is PGA Tour betting so hard?

Fields are large, so even the best player in the world loses most weeks, and outright prices are long. Finish-position and matchup markets reduce that variance, but they carry their own margins.
