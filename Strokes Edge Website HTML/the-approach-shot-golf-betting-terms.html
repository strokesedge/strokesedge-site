Golf betting terms, explained.

Every term on a golf betting slip, translated into plain English: the markets, the settlement rules and the stats, with the catch in each one spelled out.

November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Bookmakers write golf markets in a dialect of their own, and nobody hands you a translation. This glossary covers the terms you will actually meet on a PGA Tour betting slip, in plain English, with the catch in each one spelled out. Bookmark it and come back when a market looks like it is written in code. It usually is.

The markets

TermWhat it means
Outright (winner)A bet on who wins the tournament. The simplest market, and usually the hardest to hit.
Each-wayTwo bets in one stake: half on the win, half on a place (often the top 5) at a fraction of the win odds, such as one quarter. Softens the blow when your player finishes second.
Each-way termsThe place fraction and number of places a book pays, written like 1/4 odds, 5 places. Better terms matter as much as the headline price.
Top 5 / Top 10 / Top 20A bet that a player finishes inside that number. Shorter odds than the outright, far more forgiving. How ties are settled varies by book, so check for dead heat rules.
Make the cutA bet that a player survives the halfway cut and plays the weekend.
Matchup (head-to-head)Two players paired by the book. The lower score over the round or tournament wins. A tie is usually a push and your stake comes back.
Two-ball / three-ballA matchup between two or three players for one round only, usually the group they are actually teeing off in.
First round leaderA bet on who leads after Round 1. High variance, priced accordingly.
FuturesMarkets settled weeks or months later: major winners, FedEx Cup champion, player of the year.
Prop betA side market that is not about who wins: a hole in one, the winning margin, the winner's nationality.

Odds, payouts and settlements

TermWhat it means
Odds (price)What the book will pay. +2000 means 20 to 1: a $10 bet returns $210 total if it wins.
ValueWhen a player's true chance of winning is better than the odds suggest. The only reason to place a bet.
Dead heatWhen players tie for a paid place at a book that uses dead heat rules, your payout is cut: the stake is divided by the number of players tied. Two players tie for the last paid place, you get paid on half your stake.
VoidThe bet is cancelled and the stake refunded. Happens most often when a player withdraws before a tournament starts.
PushA tie in a bet with no tie option, usually a matchup. Stake returned, nobody wins.
Closing line valueWhether the odds you took beat the price at tee off. Beating the close over time is the sign of a real edge.
Vig (juice)The book's built-in margin. Golf outright books price the field to well over 100 percent, which is why casual fields are so hard to beat.
Live bettingBetting during the tournament as odds update hole by hole. Prices move fast and mistakes get punished faster.

The stats you will hear

TermWhat it means
Strokes gainedHow many strokes a player gains or loses against the field average, split into off the tee, approach, around the green and putting. The core stat of modern golf analysis.
Tee to greenOff the tee plus approach plus around the green. Everything except putting, and the most repeatable part of a player's game.
ApproachIron play into greens. In most course models it is the single best predictor of who finishes near the top.
GIRGreens in regulation: reaching the green in two shots under par or better. A blunt but useful ball-striking measure.
ProximityAverage distance left to the hole after an approach shot. Closer means more birdie putts.
ScramblingHow often a player still makes par after missing the green. A short game safety net.
Course fitHow well a player's strengths match what a specific course rewards: length, accuracy, a particular putting surface.
RegressionExtreme results drifting back toward a player's normal level. Last week's hot putter is the classic example.
Cut lineThe score needed to make the weekend, usually the top 65 and ties on the PGA Tour.
