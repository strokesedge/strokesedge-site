LIV Golf is winding down. What happens next?

Five years, billions of dollars, and a merger that never happened. The breakaway league is in a New Jersey bankruptcy court, and the only real question left is where its players land.

November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

What happened

LIV Golf is finished as a going concern. After the Saudi Public Investment Fund said on April 30, 2026 that it would fund the league only through the end of the 2026 season, LIV cut its season short in August and filed for Chapter 11 protection in New Jersey. A proposed $300 million rescue from BC Partners' credit arm, meant to relaunch the league as LIV 2.0, collapsed. On November 5, LIV's lawyer told the bankruptcy court the league would wind down, and on November 10 LIV filed its Chapter 11 plan for an orderly wind-down, according to the AP. A court hearing on the disclosure statement is set for November 24, with a final hearing proposed for January 7, 2027.

The merger that was supposed to prevent all of this never existed on paper. The framework agreement announced on June 6, 2023 was an outline, and it never became a definitive deal. Outgoing commissioner Jay Monahan told Golf Channel this month that a February 2025 White House meeting made it clear a deal would not happen. His successor, Brian Rolapp, who takes over on January 1, 2027, told Bloomberg in August that there were no merger talks. There is no negotiation left to wait for.

The only question that matters now: the players

LIV's roster still holds some of the best players in the world, and as of today none of them has a confirmed route back. The open issues, in the order they will resolve:

  • Reinstatement. The PGA Tour suspended the players who left. Whether returning players face fines, suspensions or a waiting period is the Tour's call, and it is the decision that shapes 2027 fields.
  • Money owed. The bankruptcy plan will decide what happens to outstanding guaranteed payments. That fight runs through the court, not the Tour.
  • World ranking points and majors. Where former LIV players can earn ranking points in 2027 decides who shows up at the Masters and the other majors.

Watch the November 24 hearing for the money, and Rolapp's first rulings in January for everything else.

What it means if you bet on golf

For four seasons the model treated LIV and PGA Tour golf as two separate sports, because they were: different fields, different formats, no shared baseline. StrokesEdge never blended the two stat pools, and weeks like this are why. Blended numbers built on a league that no longer exists would be worse than useless.

If even part of the LIV roster returns to PGA Tour fields in 2027, fields get deeper at the top and outright prices on the current regulars get shorter. The first few events with returning names will be the hardest to price all year, for books and models alike. That is usually where the value hides. It is also where the mistakes hide, so the honest answer is that 2027 starts with more uncertainty than any season since the split.

The split cost golf fans four years of the best players never meeting. Whatever you thought of LIV, deeper fields are better for everyone who watches, and everyone who bets.
