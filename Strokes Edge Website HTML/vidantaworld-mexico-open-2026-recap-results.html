VidantaWorld Mexico Open 2026 Recap: Novak Wins by Five, How the Model's Picks Played

Fisk T2 at 70-1, three clean fades, and a winner the model ranked 42nd. Every pick graded at Vidanta Vallarta.

Published Nov 3, 2026

Andrew Novak won the 2026 VidantaWorld Mexico Open at Vidanta Vallarta by five strokes, finishing at 25-under 259 after rounds of 67, 61, 62 and 69. Steven Fisk and Kristoffer Ventura tied for second at 20-under. Our VidantaWorld Mexico Open 2026 picks had a mixed week to grade: Steven Fisk finished T2, Doug Ghim and Trace Crowe both finished inside the top 11, all three outright fades avoided the winner, and five of our eleven positive plays missed the cut. The winner himself was a player the model did not rate. Here is the full breakdown, with the results logged on the public pick tracker and the model’s inputs in our PGA Tour betting analysis.

Novak’s second-round 61 and third-round 62 did the damage. He opened with a 67 (-4), then played the middle 36 holes in 19 under and never gave the lead back. The preview expected a low-scoring week (winning scores of -17 to -24 in past editions), and Novak’s -25 cleared the top of that range.

How the Model’s Picks Actually Played

What worked

Steven Fisk (+7000, Longshot / Value, model rank #5) was the best result on the card. Fisk finished T2 at 20-under, closing with a 63. The article flagged his 96th-percentile off-the-tee game as the engine of a +1.77% edge, and he drove it to a tied-for-second finish at 70-to-1.

Doug Ghim (+2000, E/W Winner, model rank #1) was the model’s top-ranked player and finished T6 at 18-under with a closing 69. He was seven strokes behind Novak, a top-10 finish for the player the model liked most.

Trace Crowe (+5500, Top 10/20, model rank #8) finished T11 at 17-under after a Sunday 63. That is a Top 20 finish for a player the article called out for a 98th-percentile approach game.

Tom Kim (+1800, outright fade) finished T6. The article said FADE OUTRIGHT ONLY, Top 10/20 still viable, and that is how it played: no win, but a Top 10 finish.

Ben James (+1600, outright fade) finished T16, same call, a Top 20 finish with the outright avoided.

Wyndham Clark (+1200, outright fade) missed the cut. He carried the worst edge on the board at -6.31%, and that was the clearest fade of the week.

Tom Kim over Wyndham Clark (matchup) was a clean win for the model: Kim T6, Clark missed the cut.

What did not

Johnny Keefer (+2200, E/W Winner, model rank #2) missed the cut at 2-under.

Gordon Sargent (+6500, E/W Winner, model rank #3) finished T34 at 11-under. He was the article’s biggest-edge pick at +2.71%. He made the weekend and shot 71 and 70 to finish mid-pack, not the result the number suggested.

Danny Walker (+15000, Longshot) missed the cut at +1.

Chan Kim (+10000), David Ford (+4000) and Zach Bauchou (+4500), all Top 10/20 plays, missed the cut. Bauchou also lost the matchup against Jordan Smith (Smith T16, Bauchou missed the cut).

Corey Conners (+3000, Top 10/20) finished T48 at 8-under, and Harry Hall (+3500, Top 10/20) finished T23 at 13-under, outside the Top 20 line.

The Winner the Model Didn’t Rate

Andrew Novak (+3300) was not on the picks card. His model profile shows exactly why: he ranked #42 of 103 players with a 52.6 L1 score and failed the Winner DNA gates. The model loved his approach game, which graded at a field-leading +0.902 (99th percentile), the single best number in the field on the factor with the heaviest weight. But he failed the off-the-tee gate by a hair (-0.109 against a -0.10 threshold) and the putting gate by a wide margin (-0.274 against -0.15). The gates screen out players whose putting or driving profile is a liability, and this week a player who failed two of them won by five. That is a legitimate miss on the winner and we are reporting it as one.

Ventura, who tied for second, was also a gate failure (approach -0.059 against a -0.05 threshold). Mackenzie Hughes, who tied for fourth, ranked #73 and failed the gates as well. Novak, Ventura and Hughes, three of the five players at T4 or better, all failed at least one gate. On the other side, the Top 10 also included Ghim (rank #1), Tom Kim and Fisk, all gate passers, along with Ben Griffin at T4.
