World Wide Technology Championship 2026 Picks: Model Best Bets and Fades for El Cardonal at Diamante

Approach and off-the-tee play lead the model at El Cardonal. Best bets, fades and a +10000 value play for the 2026 World Wide Technology Championship.

Published Nov 4, 2026 · El Cardonal at Diamante · Cabo San Lucas, Mexico · 2026-11-05 · Par 72 · 7,452 Yds

The 2026 World Wide Technology Championship runs 2026-11-05 at El Cardonal at Diamante, Cabo San Lucas, Mexico.

Diamante's El Cardonal setup asks a different question than most fall-swing stops, rewarding approach precision over raw distance despite the scorecard's length. The field here is thinner, the stakes lower in the standings sense, which tends to scramble the usual favorites-chalk dynamic. That matters more than people want to admit when building a model.

Model Framework

  • SG: Approach, 20%. Longer yardage means more approaches from mid-to-long range, making overall approach skill the single biggest differentiator.
  • SG: Off the Tee, 14%. A 7,452-yard track rewards players who can both find fairways and generate distance off the tee.
  • Course-Fit Approach Component, 10%. Data Golf's course-fit approach component captures how a player's specific approach profile maps onto this course's green complexes and demands.
  • Driving Distance Fit, 10%. Elite length is a real advantage on a course this long, shortening approach clubs into firm greens.
  • SG: Around the Green, 10%. Firm, exposed greens with likely run-off areas put a premium on creative short-game skill.
  • Course-Fit Short-Game Component, 8%. Course-fit short-game component reflects how a player's specific scrambling style suits this terrain.
  • SG: Putting, 8%. Putting remains a scoring separator even on a ball-striker's course.
  • Driving Accuracy Fit, 6%. Desert/coastal layouts often punish errant tee shots with native rough or waste areas, so accuracy still carries some weight.
  • Scrambling %, up-and-down rate from missed greens, 6%. Missed-green recovery rate matters on a resort course where greens can be firm and undulating.
  • Greens in Regulation %, 4%. Green-hitting frequency is a direct proxy for handling the longer approach shots this course demands.
  • Proximity 150-200yd Fairway, SG-per-shot, 4%. Given the yardage, a meaningful share of approaches will come from this longer fairway range, so proximity skill there is relevant.

Winner DNA gates this week:

  • SG: Approach >= -0.1. Approach play is central to scoring well here, so players with below-average DG approach form are unlikely contenders.
  • SG: Off the Tee >= -0.05. The course's length requires at least competent off-the-tee form to avoid being overpowered by the yardage.
  • Driving Distance Fit >= -0.05. Severe negative distance fit is a real liability on a track pushing 7,450+ yards.
  • Greens in Regulation % >= 55. A baseline green-hitting rate is needed to sustain scoring on a course where approach precision is paramount.

Weather

Wind running 8-15 mph across the tournament window, gusts up to 32 mph. Temperatures 70-85F. 1 day(s) in the window carrying meaningful rain.

Sustained wind in the 8-15 range with gusts pushing past 30 changes club selection on nearly every approach, and that's where this model's signal lives. Ball-strikers who control trajectory have an edge that doesn't show up in a simple driving-distance stat. One day of real rain softens greens briefly, which could open scoring windows early before firmness returns. Treat the wind column as the dominant variable this week, not the heat.
