Wyndham Championship 2026 Recap: A 4-for-14 Week Still Finishes Green

Sedgefield Country Club · Greensboro, North Carolina · August 6–9, 2026

Published Aug 10, 2026

Michael Brennan closed with a 64, capped by a birdie on the 18th hole, to win the Wyndham Championship at 22-under 258, three shots ahead of runner-up Beau Hossler at Sedgefield Country Club. Ben James carded the low round of the week, a final-round 61, to climb into solo third, one shot behind Hossler. It was Brennan’s second career PGA Tour win, and it doubled as his ticket into the FedExCup Playoffs, the last spot in the field went to him.

Fourteen total positions logged for the Wyndham Championship. Four cashed, all wins, no places, against nine settled losses, a 28.6% hit rate. Total staked: $146.25. Total returned: $185.36. Net result: +$39.11, a 26.7% return on money risked.

What the Model Got Right

Ben Kohles was the model’s #1 overall play heading into the week, the highest L1 score in the field and a +2.40% edge. He finished T29 at -8, exactly inside the Top 30 line his ticket needed, cashing cleanly where the model actually pointed rather than at the outright number.

The Doug Ghim and Hideki Matsuyama Top 20 parlay was the biggest single result of the week. Ghim was the model’s #9 ranked play, Matsuyama #2, both inside the model’s own top 10 for Sedgefield. Matsuyama backed that up with a T7 finish at -14, comfortably inside the number, and Ghim’s T19 at -11 got there by exactly one shot over the Top 20 cutoff line, a real result, not a photo finish that needed a favorable ruling. Ghim’s standalone Top 40 bet cashed on the same finish.

Davis Chatfield’s Top 40 ticket, a longshot/value-tier call at #21 in the model, delivered the largest per-dollar return of the week outside the parlay, a T25 finish at -9 turned a $10 stake into $36.

Where the Model Missed

Emiliano Grillo carried a +1.19% edge and the #5 model rank inside the E/W Winner tier, and missed the cut entirely, no Top 40 ticket, no points, nothing. That’s the sharpest gap this week between model conviction and result.

Ben Kohles’ own E/W Winner ticket missed even as his Top 30 line on the same player cashed, the same T29 finish that paid the finish market was never within shouting distance of the +4500 outright number. Davis Thompson (#7, +0.20% edge, T14 at -12) and Zac Blair (#8, +1.10% edge, missed cut) both sat inside the model’s top 10 plays for the week; Thompson’s finish was genuinely solid but well short of outright contention, while Blair’s week ended after 36 holes, sinking both his E/W Winner and Top 40 tickets. Davis Chatfield’s own E/W Winner and outright Winner tickets both missed the same way his teammates did, a real week for the player, not a real week for the outright number.

DFS Performance: How the Rostered Names Actually Finished

GPP lineup: Kohles ($7,700, T29 -8), Matsuyama ($9,500, T7 -14), Kim ($9,200, T5 -15), Grillo ($7,300, CUT), Rai ($8,800, T29 -8), Glover ($6,800, CUT).

Four of six made the cut, and the two studs at the top of the salary sheet delivered exactly the kind of week a GPP build is supposed to hope for: Tom Kim’s T5 and Matsuyama’s T7 were both genuinely strong, top-of-the-board finishes, not just “made the cut” results. Kohles and Rai each landed at T29, a real but modest cash-adjacent finish. Grillo and Glover, the two cheapest value plays in the build, both missed the cut and returned nothing. Net effect: a real ceiling week from the top of the roster, dragged down but not sunk by the two cheap misses at the bottom.

Cash lineup: Young ($10,400, T61 -4), Kohles ($7,700, T29 -8), Griffin ($9,100, CUT), Ghim ($7,800, T19 -11), Smith ($7,200, T14 -12), Cauley ($7,400, T54 -5).

This build’s floor-focused thesis held up in two spots and broke in two others. Jordan Smith’s T14 and Doug Ghim’s T19 were both strong, exactly the consistent production a cash roster is built to find. But Cameron Young, the single highest-salaried player in either lineup at $10,400 and the field’s top DK value score entering the week, finished T61 at just 4-under, a result that matches almost exactly what the outright betting model already flagged before the tournament started: elite ball-striking undercut by a putter that couldn’t hold up over four rounds. Ben Griffin missing the cut at $9,100 was the more expensive miss of the two, a dead roster spot at a real salary cost. Bud Cauley’s T54 rounded out the build below the pace the other five needed from him.

The Bigger Picture

A 4-for-14 week still closed at +$39.11, a 26.7% return on the $146.25 risked. That’s the value and E/W tier structure working exactly as designed: price a player like Kohles or Chatfield at long enough odds, and one or two live tickets can cover several misses elsewhere without needing a high hit rate to do it.

Worth saying plainly: none of the four cashed bets this week were on the eventual champion, and Michael Brennan wasn’t on this week’s picks card at all. The model’s edge showed up in value and cash-tier bets this week, not in calling the winner, and the numbers above are built to show that distinction rather than blur it.
