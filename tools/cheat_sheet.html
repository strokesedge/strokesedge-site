2. How to read a number

Strokes gained compares each shot with the average result from the same spot for a reference field. +0.50 per round in a category means half a stroke better than average. A negative number is worse than average.

  • Putting is the noisiest category. A hot putting week is a weaker signal than strong ball-striking.
  • Data providers use different baselines. Never mix numbers or thresholds from two sources.
  • A player with few recent rounds is unknown, not bad.

3. The odds math

Implied probability (+ odds) = 100 / (odds + 100)
Implied probability (- odds) = odds / (odds + 100)
Decimal odds D = 1 + profit / stake
Edge = your probability - implied probability
EV per $100 = 100 x (p x D - 1)

Example: +1325 is decimal 14.25, so the implied chance is 100 / 1425 = 7.0%. If you rate the player at 9%, the edge is +2.0 points and EV is +$28.25 per $100.

Every golf market adds up to more than 100%. The extra is the sportsbook's margin.

4. The weekly process

  1. Course. Work out which categories have separated the field there.
  2. Weights. Set the weight for each category and course-fit factor.
  3. Field. Score every player and apply minimum thresholds. A player who fails can be a top 10 candidate, not an outright bet.
  4. Price. Compare the model's probability with the market price. No gap, no bet.
  5. Size and log. Fixed unit, every bet recorded, losers included.

6. Common mistakes

  • Betting only favorites, whose prices already reflect their quality.
  • Trusting course history after a renovation or reroute.
  • Betting before the field and the weather are confirmed.
  • Ignoring the place terms on each-way bets.
  • Not shopping for the best price across sportsbooks.
