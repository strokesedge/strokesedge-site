## 02 — How the Model's Picks Actually Played

**LONGSHOT / VALUE.** Austin Smotherman (+10000, model rank #15) won outright. The preview flagged matching 87th percentile marks in approach and driving and called him a ball-striking-first play, with putting at 58th percentile the only middling number. A 63-62 start at a birdie-heavy, generous-fairway course is what that profile looks like when it clicks. The other longshot, Seamus Power (+8000, rank #12), finished T21 at -17. Power's 90th percentile putting was real, but his 37th percentile off the tee, the weakness the preview named, kept him outside the top 20.

**E/W WINNER.** Johnny Keefer (+3500, rank #3) finished T10 at -19, the best result of the three. His 97th percentile putting and 92nd percentile approach delivered a top-10 finish without threatening Smotherman. Ben Kohles (+4500, rank #5) finished T13 at -18, in the top 20 but not close to the lead. Max Greyserman (+4500, rank #7) missed the cut at -5. The preview called him a one-dimensional putting bet with a 56th percentile approach game, and that was the part that failed him.

**TOP 10 / TOP 20.** This was the weakest tier. Ben James (rank #2) finished T5 and Doug Ghim (rank #4) finished second, so both cashed in the finish markets. The other four did not: Jackson Koivun (rank #1) finished T65 at -8, Kevin Yu (rank #8) finished T32, Zecheng Dou (rank #9) finished T67, and Maverick McNealy (rank #6) missed the cut. That is 2 of 6 in the tier. The model's top-ranked player, with a 78.9 L1 score and a 97th percentile approach number, never got into contention, so the preview's thesis of "trust the ball-striking profile" did not hold for Koivun this week.

**FADES.** All three outright fades held. Michael Brennan (+1600) finished T13, Ben James (+1500) finished T5, and Jackson Koivun (+1200) finished T65. None won. Each fade was outright only, with Top 10/20 listed as still viable. Brennan's T13 and James's T5 would have been fine finish-market plays, and James is the clearest example of the fade-outright, play-the-finish-market split working as written. Koivun's finish-market viability did not pan out.

**MATCHUPS.** Split 1-1. Keefer over Yu was a win (T10 versus T32). Greyserman over McCarty was a loss, with Greyserman missing the cut and McCarty finishing T53 at -11.

## 03 — What the Model Got Right and What It Did Not

The Smotherman call is the one that matters for how this model is meant to work. His rank #15 sat well below the headline names, but the ball-striking inputs (approach, driving) were the two highest-weighted categories in the framework, and his putting was merely average, not a liability. Ghim (rank #4) and James (rank #2) finishing second and T5 fits the same tee-to-green-first approach.

The honest negative is Koivun. The preview framed him as a quality signal, not a value bet, and his edge was already negative at -0.68%. Even so, a #1 rank that finishes T65 is a miss on the model's highest-confidence slot, and it is worth tracking whether Black Desert's thin course history and altitude adjustment are leaving something out for young, first-time visitors.
